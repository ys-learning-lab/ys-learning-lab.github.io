Ways to use this site

Everything here is free to use, with no sign-up, for anyone. Use any tool in the way that suits you. Below are a few examples of how it is used.

A new sheet every time

Every sheet is generated when you press the button, from a random selection, so the next one is different from the last. There is always a new sheet to practise with.

That makes repeat practice easy: a short sheet every day for a month, a different test for each person in a group, a fresh crossword or word search whenever someone wants one, and answer pages that always match the sheet you just made. The sheets are plain black and white with no pictures, so they print quickly and cheaply, and the variety comes from the content.

Keeping Japanese in everyday use at home

Children with little everyday contact with Japanese can build their kana reading with a few minutes of reading and writing a day. It works at home even when the local school uses another language, or when Japanese books and workbooks are hard to find or expensive to ship.

The kana charts and writing practice sheets print at home, and the writing sheets are laid out in vertical columns that read right to left, the way Japanese is written. The on-screen games use everyday words and work on a phone or tablet. The whole site can be switched to Japanese, so parents can use it as it is.

Running a small class or group

You can make new worksheets that differ for each lesson and for each student. The settings let you match the content to the group you are teaching.

The Kana Conversion Quiz can import a class list from a spreadsheet and make one test per person, with an answer key. Printed sheets work in a room with no devices at all, and the on-screen activities work on whatever phones or tablets people already have.

Starting Japanese from the beginning

The first month goes more smoothly with an order to follow. The Kana Study Plan gives a day-by-day order and says which tool to use on each day. The charts can show romaji small under each letter, so you can check yourself without a teacher, and the quizzes bring back the letters you missed.

No account is needed, and you can practise as little or as much as you like. Print a sheet to take with you, or use the games on a phone.

Settling in to life in Japan

Quick reading and writing of kana is often expected, and parents are often learning at the same time. We believe the best shortcut is repeating practice every day. Even a few minutes, a little at a time, is what matters.

The Kana Tap Practice shows one letter and asks for the match, and a sheet printed once can be redone as a fresh one the next day. The site can be switched between English, Japanese and Chinese, so the instructions can be read in the language you prefer.

Math practice at home or in class

The Math Worksheet Generator makes practice that matches this week's topic. It makes new problems each time, with answer pages and optional QR codes that show the answers on a phone.

About writing practice

This site does not offer writing practice with a finger or a mouse. For stroke order and other repeated handwriting practice, we recommend paper and pencil, the way writing is done in everyday life. The writing sheets are made to be printed for exactly that.
