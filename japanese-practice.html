Which characters?

Script

Type or paste the kana to practice, in any order, with or without commas or spaces. You can use hiragana, katakana or romaji (for example "a, ka, ga" or "akaga"). Voiced kana like が and ぱ work, and hiragana and katakana can be mixed: kana are printed as you typed them, and romaji becomes the script chosen above. Combined sounds like ぎゃ do not fit in one box and are left out. Each character gets its own column, once.

The row and all-characters modes cover the 46 basic characters of each script. Dakuten/handakuten kana such as が and ぱ can be practiced in "Selected characters" mode (small combined sounds are not supported).

Practice word

Your own words (optional): up to 2 words, up to 5 letters each, written in the selected script (hiragana or katakana) and each containing the selected character. The long-vowel mark ー is allowed, for example ケーキ in katakana. Leave both blank to use the built-in example words.

Only the selected character is shown and every other box is left open, so students write any word that fits. A word that puts the character in a different spot is fine too. Your word (or the built-in one) is printed on page 2 as a sample answer.

Katakana has no built-in example words, so the word row is left out unless you type your own words above.

Not every character has a built-in example word (notably を and ん), so those skip the word row unless you type your own.

How the practice sheets work

Each character gets its own column of large square boxes. The top box shows a model of the character. The first box below it has the same character in faint gray to trace over. The rest are empty, so the child writes it without help. This model, then trace, then write-alone pattern is how handwriting is taught in Japanese schools.

Columns read from top to bottom and fill the page from right to left, the way Japanese is traditionally written. Each box has faint crosshair guide lines that divide it into four quarters. They help a child place and balance each stroke.

Choosing what to practice

  • Single character: for a character that's giving your child trouble. It defaults to 15 repetitions, which fills a page. You can also add an example word that uses the character, so the child practices it in a real word.
  • By row (行): kana are grouped into rows of up to five sharing a consonant, such as あ行 (あいうえお) and か行 (かきくけこ), or ア行 and カ行 in katakana. Practicing one row at a time follows the order most learners and textbooks use.
  • Selected characters: type or paste exactly the kana you want, for example the ones a student missed, including voiced kana like が. The Kana Tap Practice results page can send its missed (or all) kana here for you.
  • All 46 basic characters: a full review once every row has been introduced.

Tips for handwriting practice

  • Size matters early. Big boxes let young hands build the motion first. Precision comes later.
  • Watch the balance. Use the crosshairs to point out where strokes should start and end. あ and お, for example, differ mainly in how their parts are balanced.
  • Learn stroke order separately. These sheets don't show stroke-order arrows. Before tracing a new character, show your child its stroke order from a textbook or a trusted reference, then use these sheets for repetition.
  • A few minutes daily. One row per day, written slowly and carefully, builds better habits than a rushed page of the whole alphabet.

Frequently asked questions

Why doesn't every character have an example word?

Some hiragana, notably を and ん, almost never begin a word, so there's no natural built-in example. Katakana has no built-in example words at all. In both cases the word row is skipped unless you type your own practice word.

Does it cover katakana or voiced sounds like が?

Katakana is covered: choose Katakana at the top of "Which characters?". Voiced sounds like が and ぱ can be practiced in "Selected characters" mode: type them into the box. Small combined sounds are not supported. To practice recognizing kana, use the Kana Conversion Quiz.

What does "Hide the other letters" do?

It turns the word row into a fill-in exercise. The selected character stays visible and the other boxes are left open, so a student writes any word that fits the pattern. For example, with あ selected and the word あんこ, the student sees あ and two open boxes. The word you entered is printed on page 2 as a sample answer.

Is there an answer key?

No. There's nothing to grade on a tracing sheet. Compare your child's writing with the model character at the top of each column instead.
