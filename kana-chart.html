How to read the chart

Each column is one row of kana (行), such as あ行 or か行. Read a column from top to bottom (あ, い, う, え, お), and read the columns from right to left, starting with あ行 on the right and ending with ん on the far left. This is the order used in Japanese classrooms, and it matches the way Japanese is written in vertical lines.

The や and わ columns have empty spaces. Those sounds either aren't used in modern Japanese or are written with letters from other columns, so the spaces stay blank. ん stands on its own, since it has no vowel.

Ways to use it

  • Keep it in view. Print the chart and put it where the student practices, so each new letter can be found among the ones already learned.
  • Point and say. Set Sub 1 and Sub 2 to None, point at letters in any order, and have the student read each one out loud. Any letter they can't read is worth extra practice.
  • Compare the scripts. Put hiragana and katakana in Main and Sub 1 to see the same sound in both. Pairs that look alike, such as か and カ or り and リ, are easier to remember together.
