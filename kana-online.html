What goes here, and what stays on paper

This section is for practice that works well on a screen: recognizing letters and building words by tapping. Writing is different. A finger or a mouse is nowhere near as accurate as a pencil, so writing practice stays on printed sheets.

For the printable tools, the charts and the study plan, go to the Japanese section.

Choose an activity for the next step

Start with Tap Practice when a learner is still matching one sound to one character. Pick one or two rows and a small number of choices, then add more only when those choices feel easy. The result screen shows every answer, so it gives a clear next list: practise the missed characters again instead of starting from all 46 each time.

Use Word Puzzle when the learner can recognize several kana and is ready to build short words. The crossings make each letter matter to more than one word. Use Word Search when the learner is ready to scan a grid and read a word in a straight line. Both activities reward looking carefully at the kana, while the printable tools remain the better choice for handwriting.

A simple weekly rhythm is enough: use Tap Practice for a short check early in the week, spend another day on Word Puzzle or Word Search, then return to the missed characters. Add a Writing Practice sheet whenever a character is recognized but still hard to write. The goal is not to finish every activity at once; it is to revisit the same small group of kana until it becomes familiar.

Keep the choices connected to what was practised before. After a chart row or writing sheet, select that same row in Tap Practice. After a few short words are familiar, let the learner meet those kana again in a puzzle or search grid. If an activity feels too hard, reduce the number of rows, choices, words, or grid size rather than changing the whole routine. A small successful round gives more useful information than a long round spent guessing.
